# User manual: work from a question

This manual starts with a task, shows the action, and then explains the construct behind it. Its structure adapts Robert E. Horn's Information Mapping approach: small, labeled units with one purpose and a presentation suited to the reader's question. The [Information Mapping methodology](https://informationmapping.com/pages/information-mapping-methodology) identifies information units and types; its [type guide](https://support.informationmapping.com/hc/en-us/articles/213446789-Present-your-information-in-a-clear-and-consistent-way) distinguishes concept, structure, process, procedure, principle, and fact.

| Label in this manual | Reader question | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| **Concept** | What is it? | Short definition and example |
| **Structure** | What parts does it have? | Part/function table or diagram |
| **Process** | What happens? | Stage/result table |
| **Procedure** | How do I do it? | Numbered steps and expected result |
| **Principle** | What rule should guide my choice? | Constraint and reason |
| **Fact** | What is true in this release? | Specific behavior or limit |

## Principle: separate evidence from interpretation

An event records what happened. An interpretation records an observer's reading of it, with a model version and possible references back to earlier events. A discourse act decides what the reader encounters and when. Keeping these distinct lets a later reinterpretation change Mara's understanding without rewriting Len's earlier repair.
